Slide 5 · Strategy

Problem hook · Value · Hypothesis

Problem hook

We must solve payment default risk, high cost per claim and damage to Riverty reputation by giving consumers agency of resolving their claims independently and reliably.

Value proposition

By priotizing the consumer's power to resolve their claims with no or minimal contact to Riverty, we increase revenues from payments and reduce the agent cost per claim while giving consumers the feeling of agency over their own financial situation. Ongoing data-driven analysis of funnel adoption provide the chance to tweak the instalment funnel to perfection.

Strategic crisis (12 months if we do nothing)

If we do nothing for 12 months we will face the risk of missing revenue through payments. If we fail in collecting afterwards as well (due to low collection rate), we face the risk that clients will leave riverty since collection rate is low. Also, the high level of involvement of agents raises the cost per claim, eating up profitability.

Data-backed hypothesis

Evidence shows that consumers like Mara do not understand what an affordable instalment agreement looks like, why affordability information is requested, how recommended payment amounts are determined, and what happens if they miss a payment, combined with the fact that only 22% of portal visitors start an instalment journey and only 46% of journeys end in an agreement.

I believe that solving the trust and confidence gap in the digital instalment journey through greater transparency and explainability for shift-working consumers with variable income such as Mara will result in more consumers agreeing to and sustaining an affordable instalment plan without contacting an agent.

This will be measured by a 10 percentage point increase in Self-Service Rate (SSR). I will protect consumer fairness, responsible automation, data care, and scalability and will make a go/no-go decision after 3 months.

Persona: Mara Metric: SSR +10 pp Decision: 3 months
Moment of misery

Customers seeking an installment plan are forced to leave the self-service journey and contact Riverty through phone or email. This creates additional manual work for agents, increases backlogs and operating costs, and negatively impacts both customer satisfaction and profitability.

Slide 6 · Research

Competitive workaround · Journey map

Role · Goal

Role: A shift-working consumer whose income varies week to week, trying to manage one or more open claims entirely through the digital portal rather than by phone.

Goal: To commit to an instalment plan she's actually confident she can keep — without needing to call anyone to get there

External workaround

Call Consumer Support

Step 1 — She opens the portal but doesn't commit on the spot; she parks the decision.
Step 2 — She tries to sanity-check the number herself, informally, with whatever she has on hand.
Step 3 — She calls Daniel specifically to ask: "why this number" and "what happens if I miss a payment."
Step 4 — If she doesn't call and still doesn't get clarity, she commits anyway, essentially gambling rather than deciding.

Evidence

"It just gave me a number. I don't know why that number and not a different one, so I didn't trust it."

"Nobody told me what happens if I miss one payment versus two. I just assumed the worst."

Future-state strategy

Transparent, Adaptive Self-Service

Explain the number, show the consequence, and let her adjust the plan herself when her income changes, so trust (not pressure) drives commitment.

1 · Documented, Not Guessed 2 · No Call Required 3 · Durable, Not One-Time

Highest agent-escalation rate of any archetype · only persona tied to a confirmed post-agreement default — "resolved at creation, but didn't hold".

Stage 01

Opens portal

Receives digital reminder; starts on phone with little time to spare.

Stage 02

Income & claims

Gets through income questions and multi-claim setup willingly.

Stage 03

Blind number

Single amount, no why, no miss consequences — trust breaks; she parks or calls.

PRD highlights

She cannot agree until she has seen the difference. On the commit step, Agree stays off until she confirms she has read what one missed payment does and what a second miss does. That is the fix for "I just assumed the worst."

The explanation is short, fixed, and final. Two approved sentences, 40 words or fewer each, on her phone, for the plan amount already on screen. The amount does not change, and the screens contain no call, chat, or agent action.

Success is comprehension, then conversion. In the prototype, completers can restate one miss versus a second miss without asking. In production, the step has to lift commit-to-agreement without raising 7-day agent contact or the 19% of new agreements that miss a payment within 60 days.

Anchors

Primary metric: Self-Service Rate (SSR) — resolved with no inbound contact in the 30 days before or after resolution.

Guardrails: Fairness to consumers · Responsible automation · Data care · Scalability

Slide 8 · Validation

Experiment plan · Missed-payment explainer

Hypothesis

I believe that Missed-payment explainer for Shift worker, income varies week to week, one or more open claims, resolving them in the portal. will result in Evidence shows that consumers like Mara do not understand what an affordable instalment agreement looks like… as measured by a 3 pts change in the primary success metric within 2 weekly cycles. We will protect comprehension and amount stability throughout the test.

Primary metric

Prototype: 8/8 agree without asking what a missed payment does. Production lever vs 31% 7-day agent-contact.

Baseline

19%

new agreements miss a payment within 60 days

MDE · n · split

3 pts

100 / arm · 50/50 · α 0.04 · 2 cycles

Control (A)

Commit screen shows a single number with no reasoning and no miss explanation. Workaround: Call Consumer Support.

Variant (B)

Screen A — Plan summary (€48 / 28th / 6 / 2 claims). Agree disabled until acknowledged.
Screen B — Fixed oneMiss / secondMiss cards (≤40 words), grace period, checkbox.
Screen C — Same amount, repeated outcomes, “You reviewed this before you agreed.”

Held constant · Guardrail

No phone, chat, or agent controls; amount not editable. Consumer confidence in the decision must not decline. Completers must restate one miss vs second miss.

Ship

Primary metric improves by ≥ 3 pts at 0.04 and guardrails hold after 2 weekly cycles.

Iterate

Direction is positive but lift is below the MDE.

Kill

Primary metric shows no improvement or moves negatively. Read date fixed — no peeking.
